# Universal Tutor

**Understand the concept. Build the foundations. Connect what you learn.**

Universal Tutor is a portable set of instructions that turns an AI chat into a structured study partner. Bring your slides, notes, homework, or past exams and use them as the basis for lessons, practice, and review.

It is designed to explain **why things happen**, uncover missing prerequisites, and help you apply ideas independently. It leads one question at a time, while letting you interrupt or change direction.

**Beta 0.1 is available now.** The files are on the beta branch while the initial pull request is open.

## Start in a few minutes

1. Open the portable tutor link above and download the Markdown file using GitHub's download button, or copy its complete contents.
2. Attach the file or paste its contents into a new AI chat.
3. Add your course materials and send a request like this:

> Use these tutoring instructions. Teach me from these course files. My goal is to understand the material for my next exam. Start at my level, explain why things work, and guide me one question at a time.

No course files yet? Name a subject and goal to start. If your AI tool supports installable skills, download the ZIP and follow that tool's installation method instead.

This is a reusable instruction package. It requires no separate app, server, or API setup. Your AI tool supplies the model and file-reading capabilities.

## What a lesson should feel like

The tutor establishes your goal and starting point, explains a manageable concept, demonstrates it, and gradually hands more of the work to you.

If a missing foundation blocks progress, it explains why a detour will help, teaches the prerequisite, and returns to the original lesson. You can skip a detour, though the gap stays unresolved.

As you progress, it creates opportunities to notice connections to other ideas within your course. Hints provide support while leaving room for you to make the connection yourself. Independent application and later review help check understanding beyond a single correct answer.

## What it can help with

- **Deep explanations:** meaning, reasoning, worked examples, and when an idea applies.
- **Course-based study:** lessons grounded in readable slides, notes, homework, readings, rubrics, and past exams.
- **Adaptive practice:** support and difficulty adjusted to your answers and recurring mistakes.
- **Missing foundations:** targeted prerequisite lessons with a return to the original topic.
- **Learning plans:** priorities based on your goals, available time, and exam date.
- **Review and reinforcement:** revisit earlier material and check independent understanding.
- **Progress handoffs:** save a learner record for your next chat.

## Subjects and study modes

Subject modes cover mathematics, statistics, natural sciences, history and social studies, literature and humanities, writing, languages, programming, applied subjects, and creative or practical skills. Modes can be combined for interdisciplinary topics.

| Study mode | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Guided | Learn with explanations, examples, and gradually reduced support. |
| Practice | Work through problems with feedback and targeted help. |
| Quiz | Check understanding with assessment tasks. |
| Exam | Attempt an assessment without hints or immediate feedback until it ends. |
| Review | Revisit earlier learning, recurring errors, and topics due for another check. |

You do not need to choose a mode before starting. Describe your goal and redirect the tutor whenever you need to.

## Things you can ask

> “I don't understand why this works. Explain the foundations before giving me another problem.”

> “Teach me from these biology slides, then use this past exam to guide fresh practice.”

> “Help me analyze this historical source. Let me build the argument instead of writing it for me.”

> “Use language mode for guided Spanish conversation. Help me understand my recurring mistakes.”

> “My exam is in six days and I have 30 minutes each evening. Make a realistic plan.”

> “Review what we learned yesterday, then help me connect it to today's topic.”

## Save your progress

Before leaving, ask: **“Give me a portable learner record.”** Save it separately. In a new chat, provide the tutor instructions, that record, and any course files needed for the next lesson.

The skill does not create automatic cross-chat memory or scheduled reminders. Keep personal records and course uploads out of this public repository.
