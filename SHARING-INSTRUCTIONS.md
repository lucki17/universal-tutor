# Share the Universal AI Tutor — Beta 0.1

Release: October 17, 2026. The ZIP now includes `GETTING-STARTED.md`; the portable file includes the same guide. Share this labeled release so feedback can identify the version used. This is a beta, not a validated guarantee of teaching quality or learning outcomes.

This is a teaching skill, not an application. It requires no server, subscription integration, or API key beyond whatever AI tool the recipient already uses.

## With a tool that supports skills

Share `universal-ai-tutor.zip`. It contains the `universal-ai-tutor` folder, with `SKILL.md`, `GETTING-STARTED.md`, a learner-record reference, and optional Codex display metadata. The recipient can add that folder using their tool's supported skill-import or skill-installation mechanism. Support and installation locations vary by tool.

Once available, a starter request is:

> Use universal-ai-tutor to help me learn [subject]. My goal is [goal]. Start at my level and ask one question at a time.

## With an ordinary AI chat

Share `UNIVERSAL-AI-TUTOR-PORTABLE.md`. The recipient can paste its contents into a new chat or attach it and ask:

> Use these instructions as my tutoring approach. I want to learn [subject]. Teach me one question at a time.

The portable version includes the learner-record format, so it does not depend on other files. The receiving tool's own instructions and capabilities still apply. A chat prompt cannot create durable memory, scheduled reminders, or guaranteed answer accuracy.

## Study from your own files

The tutor includes subject modes for mathematics, statistics, natural sciences, history, literature, writing, languages, programming, applied subjects, and creative/practical skills. It selects one from your request or materials, and can combine them. Subject modes work alongside guided, practice, quiz, exam, and review activities: for example, “Use history mode and quiz me on these sources” or “Use language mode for guided Spanish conversation.” You do not need to select a mode before starting.

Attach your lecture slides, past exams, homework, notes, readings, or rubrics, then ask:

> Use universal-ai-tutor to teach me from these files. My goal is to prepare for my next exam. Identify what I need to learn, check my understanding, and tutor me one question at a time. Use the past exams to guide the style of fresh practice questions.

The tutor will use accessible files to build a course map, explain the relevant concepts, and tailor practice to your material. It should cite actual slides, pages, or questions; distinguish imported questions from generated practice; and flag missing or unreadable content. File-reading capabilities depend on the receiving AI tool. If a format is unsupported, provide a readable export or excerpt. The skill does not itself add a file parser.

You can also ask: “Help me with homework question 4 without giving away the solution,” “Quiz me on slides 10–20,” or “Review the mistakes in my marked exam.”

The skill also supports “I'm lost” resets, occasional confidence checks, teach-back tasks, a study schedule around your exam deadline, and reserving fresh questions for later assessment. For example: “My exam is in six days and I have 30 minutes each evening. Make a realistic plan and keep some questions unseen for a final check.” These are tutoring instructions, not automatic reminders or guarantees of exam performance.

## Carrying progress between chats

The tutor leads short detours when a missing foundation blocks understanding: it explains why the detour is needed, teaches the foundation, and returns to the original lesson. You can ask to skip, although it will explain why that is not recommended and retain the gap as unresolved. It also weaves connections between ideas within your course into lessons, giving you a chance to recognize the relationship before supplying progressively clearer help.

At the end of a study session, ask:

> Give me a portable learner record so I can continue with another tutor.

Share that separate record only when you intend to transfer that learner's progress. The reusable skill itself should stay free of personal learner data.
